The story so far.

October 1, 2026

Origin story

The year was 1992. On a warm May day, my saint of a mother pushed me out of her womb and I was thrust into the world with my eyes shining with innocence and hopes and dreams.

Fast forward years later, dad brought home a computer and I was hooked. Videogames held my attention for hours and I somehow managed to sit still until I grew up enough to go to school. I’d spend hours with Doom, Diablo, Warcraft II, and messing around in DOS and paint.

Childhood computing

I’d have computer classes in school - Word and Powerpoint were my first victims. Free time would be spent creating animations or playing with fonts and colors and laughing at all the bad words I could type.

Then came highschool - and my first exposure into programming. I took to Visual Basic like a fledgling jumping off a branch. I’d read the textbook for fun, and follow along with the activities in my idle time. Made mistakes like having white text on white background, inadvertent infinite loops, and I even got entered into a competition at some point. Friendster came and went, Tumblr moved in and out of vogue, and online MMORPGs were becoming a thing.

Baby steps into web development

Choosing a course for college was a no-brainer. Something computer related. I applied and was accepted into college in the Information and Communications Technology Bachelor of Science program. I’d eventually join the school’s student IT organization and continue to develop small applications which could be used by the general public - mostly barcode reader driven attendance and registration systems for events. This was my first foray into web development as well. Facebook made its debut as a startup, twitter became a thing, and Google explicitly told us they weren’t going to be evil (oh where has the time gone?)

Graduation came and went, and I was quickly inducted into the Philippine workforce.

The first few jobs

Eager to prove myself, I joined the team at C3 Interactive - a marketing agency with Philippine clients who needed webpages made. Under the tutelage of the Senior Programmer, I took on tasks that would shape my career. I took what I knew of PHP from college lessons and started using frameworks - abstraction on top of the procedural logic I’ve gotten used to. I didn’t like it at first - but what did I know back then, eh?

The year passed in a whirlwind of CRUD apps, HTML slicing, and a bit of server maintenance here and there. I picked up a bit of linux experience, some production MySQL knowledge, and MVC I eventually moved on to try a support role - just so I could experience it. I spent days and nights on a shifting schedule chatting with international staff, guiding them through managing their outlook settings and resetting passwords The combination of the different workflow with the shifting schedule didn’t agree with me, so I left again to search for career advancement and skill growth elsewhere.

The Logistics system

With the meager bit of experience under my belt, I joined a local trucking and shipping logistics company - Avega Brothers Integrated Shipping Corp - to help digitize their existing paper-dependent system. Our team of 3 would regularly meet with the department we were targeting at the time to discuss their needs, pain points, and processes - and we started with the human resources to streamline their record-keeping with a few CRUD pages for employee information.

When that was done, we moved on to payroll - a different beast altogether. After we finished with them, a single click was the process for generating payroll of the entire company. This included tax computations, custom formulas for calculating contributions to the co-op, and handling for the aforementioned employee information (dependents, tax brackets, and so forth).

The purchasing and procurement department soon received the same treatment; we digitized tracking supplier quotations, implemented approval processes for purchases, and tracked inventory of boat and truck parts.

Since we were already overseeing a lot of the company’s finances, we tackled the accounting and finance departments next. Collaborated with the departments to build their chart of accounts, collected the necessary transaction information, and presented reports in such a way that management would appreciate.

Finally we would finish the system with the operations and marketing departments - mostly tracking, storing, and retrieving active contracts and location tracking for vessels within cellphone signal range.

All of the above was built on a system where we continually improved and optimized the infrastructure; authentication, authorization, access control, gave internal seminars on email security, implemented a ticketing system, and created an environment conducive to the modern office.

In between all these tasks, the team would be often called upon for usual and not-so-usual office IT support tasks - resetting passwords, managing hardware, digital inventories, resetting the wifi, installing cameras (both CCTV and dashcam), downloading movies for the owner, and generally bolstering the digital capabilities of the staff.

It was around this time I got my first tattoo, and met my wife for the first time. I’d use my domain to host applications for our private use - a message repository, location tracker, and media host.

Managing a team

With the logistics system done, I was hungry for new and different opportunities. I was eventually onboarded to another local IT support company, but as a programmer instead of support staff. We would plan out and prototype solutions, but were ultimately met with adoption and integration issues.

It was here I was placed in charge of juniors of my own - and while our impact on the business was not as good as I’d hoped, I did pick up some nuances on how to handle a small team, sharpen skills in product presentation, and generally continue exploring tech outside conventional computing.

In spite of our challenges with implementation, we would work closely with the marketing department to build event registration forms, digital raffles, and extend support around the office that would ease the burden on administrative tasks.

It was around this time I made the switch on my laptop to linux, picked up a raspberry pi to experiment with hardware, and brushed up on soft skills to keep the department running.

Defined specifications, mature processes

An opportunity presented itself in the form of another offer which I accepted. Technopian - the company I moved to - built, customized, and maintained education systems for Japanese clients. The structure and order in the development process is one of the things that caught my interest, since it was a nice change of pace writing code with the requirements in black-and-white.

I would spend my years there reading and understanding translated spec documents and implementing the logic, patterns, and behavior of different schools in Japan. I quickly adapted to the custom framework they were using and applied client-specific business rules through inheritance of the PHP classes.

It was around this time that COVID hit - which is when we made the shift to remote work. Being one of the people living close to the office, I took on part of the responsibility to travel to the physical office to reboot crashed machines, suggest network stability improvements, and implement remote tools for administration and general use.

I also took especial notice of my team leads. How they handled disputes, disagreements, and took care of the well-being, camaraderie, and general vibe of the staff - and still bring their energy with me to this day.

It was during this time I’d have accumulated 10 years of experience, built a point-of-sale system for the family business, moved out of my parents house, and got married.

Development modernization

The theme of my previous years of experience has been primarily PHP. I wanted to change that - so I accepted a mid-level software engineer role at Pulsar-SD. Out of my element, I pushed hard to be able to catch up to peers when it came to Javascript, frontend frameworks, and the sheer mountain of technological backlog that comes from doing nothing but PHP for a decade.

With a bit of help from my peers, I helped build node application plugins that handled mail delivery, picked up Docker containerization, improved networking knowledge with DNS, touched on security with digital encryption and cryptographic document signing - all just for one of the projects with this company.

The bulk of my time here was spent creating microservices and custom hooks for customer activity for a digital banking personalization and engagement platform. I would collaborate with our team to plan out and build budgeting and subscription management modules, transaction hooks to manipulate data and generate customer-facing notifications according to business rules, and generally provide support to the development team by building internal tools to reduce friction when processing our workload.

That same fintech client would also give us additional projects that involved managing message queues. Event-driven architecture with Kafka was studied, along with Java Springboot to fulfill design requirements and ensure the message pipeline could be deployed on their infrastructure.

They invested heavily in our growth, and I was also exposed to no-code tools (Flutterflow), Angular, Firebase/Supabase, and a slew of different technologies. I built my homelab, started self-hosting applications, and set up my local LLM - all of which were used to provide additional support to our development workflow.

What’s next?

So now you’ve got context on who I am. I hope you enjoyed the read and will continue looking into the rest of my journey. Hopefully I’ll have written more by the time you visited.
